# Vegetarian Indian Air-Fryer Recipes

Ten recipes for snacks and dry vegetable dishes. All are vegetarian and contain no eggs; paneer and yogurt are used in the tikka. Quantities make small household batches, but basket capacity determines how many rounds you need.

The directions are summarized and adapted from the references beneath each recipe. Changes in seasoning, portion size or cooking settings are noted. These adaptations have not been kitchen-tested here; use the doneness checks as well as the timer.

## Before You Start

- **Measure consistently:** 1 cup = 240 ml; 1 tbsp = 15 ml; 1 tsp = 5 ml. Spoon flour into the cup and level it; do not pack it.
- **Select Air Fry mode.** Preheat at the recipe temperature according to your model's manual. Listed times exclude preheating and preparation.
- **Use one layer:** leave gaps around patties and paneer. A full recipe may require several batches. Doubling ingredients does not mean doubling cooking time.
- **Dry ingredients well:** surface moisture makes chickpeas, cauliflower and bhindi steam instead of brown.
- **Measure oil:** the amounts below are for the entire recipe, including brushing unless stated otherwise. Brush lightly rather than repeatedly adding oil.
- **Check early:** times vary with model and food size. At the first check, shake or turn as directed; finish in 1–2 minute intervals if needed.
- **Temperature units:** Celsius/Fahrenheit pairs are rounded. Choose the nearest setting; 180°C is about 355°F, not 180°F.
- **Keep airflow clear:** use accessories permitted by your model. Never preheat with loose parchment, and do not wrap the entire grate in foil. Use an approved fine-mesh insert if nuts would fall through.

Start with peanuts or corn, then try paneer and vegetables. Coated snacks and dough need more attention to thickness and moisture.

## 1. Whole Roasted Peanuts

### Ingredients

- 1 cup raw shelled peanuts
- A pinch of salt, optional

### Steps

1. Preheat to **160°C / 320°F**.
2. Spread peanuts in one layer, using an approved insert if needed to keep them from falling through.
3. Cook **6–8 minutes**, shaking twice. Inspect a split peanut for a lightly golden interior; stop before it darkens deeply.
4. Spread on a plate and cool fully before testing crunch.
5. Rub off the skins if desired. Keep peanuts whole for snacking or your peanut ladoos.

**Troubleshooting:** warm peanuts can seem soft. Cool a sample before extending the roast.

Reference: [Indian Veggie Delight — roasted peanuts](https://www.indianveggiedelight.com/air-fryer-roasted-peanuts/).

## 2. Masala Chickpeas

### Ingredients

- 1 can chickpeas, 425 g / 15 oz before draining; or about 1½ cups cooked chickpeas
- 1½ tsp oil
- ½ tsp mild Kashmiri chilli powder
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- ¼ tsp salt
- ¼ tsp roasted cumin powder, optional finish

### Steps

1. Rinse and drain cooked chickpeas. Pat dry, then spread on a towel for **30–40 minutes** until the surfaces are dry. Discard loose skins.
2. Toss with oil, chilli, garlic and salt.
3. Preheat to **200°C / 390°F** and spread in one layer.
4. Cook **13–15 minutes**, shaking two or three times. Check from 10 minutes.
5. Cool a few chickpeas and bite one: the center should be crisp rather than moist. Extend briefly if needed.
6. Add optional cumin and serve. Make small batches for best crunch.

**Important:** start with fully cooked chickpeas, not soaked raw chickpeas.

Adapted from [Cook With Manali — air-fryer chickpeas](https://www.cookwithmanali.com/air-fryer-chickpeas/), with Kashmiri chilli and optional cumin for Indian seasoning.

## 3. Besan-Coated Masala Cashews

### Ingredients

- 1 cup raw cashews, about 150 g
- 1 tsp oil
- ½ cup besan
- 1 tbsp rice flour
- 1 tsp ginger-garlic paste
- 1½ tsp mild Kashmiri chilli powder
- ⅛ tsp turmeric
- 1 tsp chaat masala
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- ¼–½ tsp salt, depending on the chaat masala
- About 2 tbsp water, added gradually

### Steps

1. Coat cashews with oil. Mix the remaining dry ingredients and paste separately, then toss with the nuts.
2. Add water by the teaspoon until the flour clings to individual nuts. Avoid a puddle of batter.
3. Preheat to **165°C / 325°F**. Arrange in one layer.
4. Cook about **10 minutes**, shaking halfway and checking from 8 minutes.
5. Cool a sample. The coating should be dry and cooked through, without raw flour flavor. Extend briefly if needed.
6. Cool the entire batch before storing.

Adapted from [Cook With Manali — masala cashews](https://www.cookwithmanali.com/air-fryer-masala-cashews/), with a lower optional salt starting point.

## 4. Onion Pakoda

### Ingredients

- 1 large onion, about 250 g, thinly sliced
- ½ cup besan
- 1 tbsp rice flour
- 3 tbsp chopped coriander leaves
- 1 chopped green chilli, optional
- ¼ tsp each ajwain, turmeric and red chilli powder
- ½ tsp coriander powder
- A pinch of garam masala
- ½ tsp salt
- 1 tsp oil for the mixture, plus about 1 tsp for brushing
- Water as needed, up to approximately 3–5 tbsp

### Steps

1. Massage salt into the separated onion slices. Rest **10 minutes**.
2. Mix in flours, spices, coriander and the first teaspoon of oil. Add water only as needed for a thick, clinging coating.
3. Form **7–8 slightly flattened clusters**, keeping them no thicker than about 1.5 cm.
4. Preheat to **170°C / 340°F**. Lightly grease the basket and space the clusters apart.
5. Cook **10 minutes**. Brush with the remaining oil, then cook at **200°C / 400°F for 4–5 minutes**.
6. Break one open to check that the center is cooked, with no wet flour. Serve hot.

Adapted from [Cook With Manali — onion pakoda](https://www.cookwithmanali.com/air-fryer-onion-pakoda/); salt and cluster thickness are adjusted here.

## 5. Aloo Tikki

### Ingredients

- 500 g boiled potatoes, drained, cooled and peeled
- 4–5 tbsp rice flour
- 1 tsp grated ginger
- 2 tbsp chopped coriander leaves
- 1 chopped green chilli, optional
- ½ tsp each chilli powder, roasted cumin, garam masala and amchoor
- ½ tsp chaat masala, optional
- ¼ tsp salt to start; adjust for the chaat masala
- About 2 tsp oil for basket and brushing

### Steps

1. Mash potatoes smoothly. Mix with spices, coriander and 4 tbsp rice flour. Add the last tablespoon only if needed for binding.
2. Shape into **8–9 patties, about 1–1.25 cm thick**. Chill **30 minutes** if soft.
3. Preheat to **190°C / 375°F**. Lightly oil the basket and patty tops.
4. Cook **12–15 minutes**, turning carefully halfway and brushing the second side.
5. Remove when a crust forms and the center is hot. Serve immediately with green chutney.

**Troubleshooting:** drain potatoes well before cooling. Adding lots of flour to wet potatoes can make dense tikkis.

Adapted from [Indian Veggie Delight — aloo tikki](https://www.indianveggiedelight.com/aloo-tikki-recipe/), with measured brushing oil and a suggested salt starting point.

## 6. Paneer Tikka

### Ingredients

- 225 g paneer, cut into roughly 3 cm cubes
- ½ cup onion chunks, layers separated
- ½ cup capsicum chunks
- ¼ cup thick plain yogurt
- 1 tsp each grated ginger and minced garlic
- 1½ tsp lemon juice
- 1 tsp mild Kashmiri chilli powder
- ¼ tsp turmeric
- ½ tsp each garam masala and coriander powder
- ¼–½ tsp salt
- ¾ tsp crushed kasuri methi
- About 1 tsp oil for basket and brushing

### Steps

1. Mix yogurt, ginger, garlic, lemon and seasonings.
2. Gently coat paneer and vegetables; refrigerate **30 minutes**.
3. Preheat to **180°C / 355°F**. Lightly grease the basket and arrange pieces with gaps; skewers are optional.
4. Brush lightly with oil. Cook **5 minutes**, turn, then cook approximately **5 more minutes**; check from 8 minutes total.
5. Serve when edges brown but paneer remains soft. Add chutney and lemon.

**Troubleshooting:** use thick yogurt and let excess marinade drip off. Do not keep cooking soft paneer merely to darken every side.

Adapted from [Piping Pot Curry — paneer tikka](https://pipingpotcurry.com/paneer-tikka/), approximately half-sized with adjusted seasonings.

## 7. Aloo Gobi

### Ingredients

- 4 cups cauliflower florets, about 550 g
- 1 cup peeled potato cubes, cut about 1 cm across
- ½ cup sliced onion
- ½ cup chopped tomato
- 2 tbsp oil
- ½ tsp each garlic powder, cumin powder, turmeric and Kashmiri chilli powder
- 1 tsp coriander powder
- ½–¾ tsp salt
- 1 tsp lemon juice and chopped coriander to finish

### Steps

1. Dry washed vegetables. Keep potatoes smaller than the cauliflower florets.
2. Toss vegetables with oil and seasonings.
3. Preheat to **195°C / 380°F**. Arrange a single layer; expect several batches in a small fryer.
4. Cook **12–15 minutes**, shaking halfway. Pierce a potato cube to test the center.
5. If potatoes remain firm, remove cooked cauliflower and continue potatoes in 1–2 minute intervals; allow up to about **18 minutes**, or longer as needed.
6. Finish with lemon and coriander.

**Troubleshooting:** if potatoes repeatedly lag behind, cook them alone for a few minutes before adding the other vegetables next time.

Adapted from [Piping Pot Curry — roasted aloo gobi](https://pipingpotcurry.com/roasted-aloo-gobi/), with a lower salt starting point and extra doneness checks.

## 8. Bharwa Bhindi / Stuffed Okra

### Ingredients

- 230–250 g tender bhindi, about 20 small pods
- 3 tbsp ground roasted peanuts
- 1 tbsp coriander powder
- 1 tsp cumin powder
- ½–1 tsp chilli powder
- ¼ tsp turmeric
- ½ tsp each garam masala and amchoor
- ½ tsp fennel powder, optional
- ¼–½ tsp salt
- 1 tbsp oil for stuffing, plus up to 2 tsp for brushing

### Steps

1. Wash bhindi and dry thoroughly. Trim ends and slit lengthwise without cutting right through.
2. Mix the peanut powder, spices, salt and tablespoon of oil. Divide between the pods; do not force them open widely.
3. Preheat to **180°C / 355°F**. Place in one layer and brush lightly with the remaining oil.
4. Cook **10–12 minutes**, turning gently at about 8 minutes.
5. Check that pods are tender and stuffing smells toasted. Extend briefly if needed and serve with roti or dal-rice.

**Note:** peanuts are ground for this stuffing only. The roasting recipe above keeps peanuts whole.

Adapted from [Indian Veggie Delight — bharwa bhindi](https://www.indianveggiedelight.com/bharwa-bhindi-stuffed-okra/), with adjustable salt and chilli.

## 9. Masala Corn on the Cob

### Ingredients

- 2 medium fresh corn cobs, husks and silk removed
- 2 tsp oil
- 1–2 tsp lemon juice
- ¼ tsp salt
- ¼ tsp chilli powder
- ¼ tsp roasted cumin powder, optional

### Steps

1. Cut cobs in half crosswise if needed to fit. Pat dry and brush with oil.
2. Preheat to **200°C / 400°F**. Arrange in one layer.
3. Cook **13–15 minutes**, turning halfway. Check from 12 minutes for tender kernels and browned spots.
4. Remove with tongs. Mix lemon, salt, chilli and optional cumin; rub or brush onto the hot corn.
5. Serve promptly. This gives roasted corn; it does not reproduce the smoky flavor of a charcoal fire.

Adaptation: cooking schedule from [Hamilton Beach — air-fryer corn](https://hamiltonbeach.ca/air-fryer-buttery-corn-on-the-cob); oil quantity and Indian lemon-spice finish are changed here.

## 10. Half-Atta Savory Diamonds

### Ingredients

- ½ cup maida and ½ cup atta
- 1 tbsp oil for dough, plus up to 1 tsp for brushing
- ¼ tsp salt
- ½ tsp ajwain or cumin seeds
- ½ tsp chilli powder, optional
- A pinch of turmeric, optional
- Water as needed; start with 3 tbsp

### Steps

1. Combine flours, salt and seasonings. Rub in the tablespoon of oil.
2. Add water gradually for a firm, pliable dough; cover for **20 minutes**.
3. Roll evenly **2–3 mm thick**, prick lightly and cut into diamonds.
4. Preheat to **180°C / 355°F**. Space pieces in one layer and brush both sides lightly.
5. Test a small batch. Check from **6 minutes**; expect approximately **8–12 minutes**, turning halfway. This thinner, reduced-oil adaptation needs a trial in your model.
6. Cool a piece and check its center is dry and crisp; extend briefly if needed.

Full recipe: [spicy-salty-maida-diamonds.md](spicy-salty-maida-diamonds.md). For the sweet syrup-coated version, see [sweet-maida-diamonds.md](sweet-maida-diamonds.md).

Technique reference: [Piping Pot Curry — namak para](https://pipingpotcurry.com/namak-para/); this uses different thickness, oil quantity and starting settings.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely reason | Adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food stays pale and soft | Crowding or excess surface moisture | Dry well and use smaller batches |
| Outside burns before center cooks | Pieces too thick, or heat too high | Cut evenly; lower heat by about 10°C and allow more time |
| Coating blows off | Loose dry flour or poorly attached coating | Add only enough moisture to make coating cling before cooking |
| Pakoda spreads | Batter too wet | Add besan a teaspoon at a time; form compact shallow clusters |
| Tikki breaks when turned | Wet potatoes or turning before crust sets | Chill shaped patties; wait for a crust and use a thin spatula |
| Paneer becomes rubbery | Overcooking | Use larger cubes and stop once lightly browned |
| Nuts seem soft straight away | Still warm | Cool a sample before adding cooking time |
| Diamonds are hard rather than crisp | Thick dough or too little oil in dough | Roll evenly and retain the specified tablespoon of oil |

## Serving, Storage and Reheating

- Serve pakoda, tikki, paneer and vegetables freshly cooked for best texture.
- Cool dry nuts and diamonds completely before sealing airtight. Make small batches and label the cooking date.
- Refrigerate cooked vegetables, patties and paneer promptly; follow usual leftover food-handling guidance.
- Reheat refrigerated cooked snacks in a single layer, checking frequently until hot throughout. Add chutney, yogurt and lemon after reheating.
- Measure salt and oil before cooking; use lemon, herbs and spices for extra flavor. Air-frying changes the cooking method, but nuts, dough snacks and sweet coatings still benefit from modest portions.
