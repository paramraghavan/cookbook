# Boondi Laddu

## Ingredients

### Boondi

* **1¼ cups fine besan** — about 160 g
* **¾ cup water** — about 175–180 ml, added gradually
* Oil or ghee for frying

### Sugar Syrup

* **1¼ cups sugar** — about 260 g
* **⅔ cup water** — about 150 ml
* 3–4 cloves
* ½ tsp cardamom powder — optional

### For Shaping

* 1–2 tsp ghee

---

# 1. Make the Boondi Batter

1. Add besan to a bowl.
2. Add about **150 ml water** and whisk until smooth and lump-free.
3. Rest for **15 minutes**.
4. Add more water, **1 tbsp at a time**, until the batter flows easily through the boondi jhara.

Total water will usually be about **175–180 ml**.

### Batter Check

Make a few test boondis before frying a full batch.

* **Round boondi → Correct**
* **Boondi with tails → Batter too thick** → add a little water
* **Flat/joined boondi → Batter too thin** → add a little besan

> The batter should fall through the jhara as individual drops, not pour through in a stream.

---

# 2. Fry the Boondi

Heat oil or ghee over **medium to medium-high heat**.

### Oil Temperature

Aim for approximately:

**350–360°F / 175–182°C**

### No-Thermometer Oil Test

Drop **one small drop of batter** into the oil.

**Correct temperature**

* Drops slightly into the oil
* Rises to the surface within about **1–2 seconds**
* Bubbles actively around it
* Keeps a **light yellow color** and does not brown immediately

**Oil too cold**

* Batter stays at the bottom
* Takes several seconds to rise
* Boondi may become flat, oily, or stick together

**Fix:** Increase the heat slightly and test again.

**Oil too hot**

* Batter shoots to the surface immediately
* Browns very quickly
* Outside cooks before the inside

**Fix:** Lower the heat and wait briefly before testing again.

> **Easy rule:** The boondi should **rise quickly and bubble actively without browning**.

### Fry

1. Hold the jhara about **3–4 inches above the oil**.
2. Pour some batter onto the jhara and gently tap so individual drops fall into the oil.
3. Do not overcrowd the pan.
4. Fry briefly. At the correct oil temperature, boondi cooks **very quickly**.
5. Remove as soon as it is cooked:

   * Soft and slightly squishy
   * No wet batter inside
   * Pale yellow to very lightly golden
   * **Not crisp or crunchy**
6. Drain and repeat.

> **Important:** Do **not** wait until all bubbling completely stops. By then the boondi may become too crisp for ladoo.

### Between Batches

Oil temperature can change after each batch.

Before adding more batter:

* Let the oil return to **350–360°F / 175–182°C**, or
* Drop in one test drop and check that it **rises quickly without browning**.

> **For ladoo:** Soft boondi absorbs syrup much better than crispy boondi.

#### Notes – Making Good Boondi

<details>
<summary><strong>Click for detailed tips</strong></summary>

* **Batter consistency:** Batter should fall through the jhara drop by drop.

  * Too thick → tailed or hard boondi
  * Too thin → flat, joined, or irregular boondi

* **Oil depth:** Use enough oil for the boondi to float freely. Shallow oil can produce flat or joined boondi.

* **Oil temperature:** The batter needs enough heat to set its round shape quickly. If the oil is too cool, boondi may stick, flatten, or form lumps.

* **Frying:** Remove the boondi while it is still soft. Do not fry until crisp.

* Wipe or clean the bottom of the jhara between batches if batter begins blocking the holes.

</details>

# 3. Make the Sugar Syrup

> [candy_thermometer_test.md](candy_thermometer_test.md)

Add to a wide pan:

* **260 g sugar**
* **150 ml water**
* 3–4 cloves

1. Heat over medium heat.
2. Stir until the sugar completely dissolves.
3. Once dissolved, avoid unnecessary stirring.
4. Boil until the syrup reaches **one-string consistency**.

### One-String Test

1. Put a small drop of syrup on a spoon.
2. Let it cool for **5–10 seconds**.
3. Lightly wet your thumb and index finger.
4. Touch a tiny amount of syrup between them.
5. Slowly pull your fingers apart.

**Ready:** One thin string forms before breaking.

> Never touch boiling syrup directly from the pan.

Optional thermometer guide:

**About 220–225°F / 104–107°C**

Once the one-string stage is reached:

1. Turn off the heat.
2. Add cardamom, if using.
3. **Do not add extra water.**

#### Notes – Sugar Syrup

<details>
<summary><strong>Click for detailed tips</strong></summary>

The syrup consistency is critical:

* **Too thin:** Boondi absorbs syrup but the ladoos remain wet and may not hold together.
* **Correct:** Syrup coats and binds the boondi while remaining moist.
* **Too thick:** Sugar may crystallize around the boondi and make the ladoos dry or hard.

Use the **one-string test** as the main indicator rather than bubble size alone.

</details>

---

# 4. Mix Boondi Into the Hot Syrup

1. Immediately add the fried boondi to the **hot syrup**.
2. Mix gently for **30–60 seconds** until evenly coated.
3. Cover and rest for **5 minutes**.

Remember:

> **HOT SYRUP → ADD BOONDI**

---

# 5. Cool Until Warm

After 5 minutes:

1. Uncover and gently stir.
2. Let the mixture cool until **warm and comfortable to handle**.

If it is too hot, spread it briefly on a wide steel tray or plate.

Do **not** let it cool completely before shaping.

Remember:

> **WARM BOONDI → SHAPE LADDUS**

---

# 6. Shape the Laddus

1. Lightly grease your palms with ghee.
2. Take a handful of the warm boondi mixture.
3. **Squeeze firmly 3–4 times**.
4. Once it holds together, gently roll it into a ball.
5. Repeat.

> **Squeeze first → then roll**

Let the finished laddus cool completely at room temperature.

---

# Quick Troubleshooting

### Boondi is flat or sticking together

Oil may be **too cool**, batter may be too thin, or oil may be too shallow.

### Boondi browns immediately

Oil is **too hot**. Lower the heat before the next batch.

### Boondi is hard/crispy

It was fried too long. Remove the next batch sooner.

### Ladoo mixture is too wet

Warm over **very low heat for 30–60 seconds**, mix, rest briefly, then shape.

### Mixture is dry or crystallizing

Add **1 tsp hot water**, mix gently, and test again.

### Mixture became too cold

Warm over very low heat for **20–30 seconds**, then shape while warm.

### Laddus still fall apart

Lightly crush about **½ cup of the syrup-coated boondi**, mix it back in, and shape again. The smaller pieces help bind the ladoos.

→ Flowing batter
→ Test for round boondi

→ Test drop rises in 1–2 seconds
→ Fry briefly
→ Keep boondi soft

**260 g sugar + 150 ml water**

> **ROUND, SOFT BOONDI → ONE-STRING SYRUP → MIX WHILE HOT → SHAPE WHILE WARM**
