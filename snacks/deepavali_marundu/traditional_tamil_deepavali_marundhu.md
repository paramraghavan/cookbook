# Traditional Tamil Brahmin Deepavali Marundhu (தீபாவளி மருந்து)

A traditional Tamil Iyer/Iyengar-style **Deepavali Marundhu (Legiyam)** made with ginger, dry ginger, long pepper, aromatic roots, jaggery, and ghee. Family recipes vary; this is one home-style version rather than a universal formula.

- **Preparation and cooking:** 45–60 minutes (approximately)
- **Yield:** Approximately 300–350 g, depending on evaporation
- **Diet:** Vegetarian; no onion or garlic

## Fastest stovetop version — about 5–7 minutes

For a tiny batch, use ready-ground spices and fine, ready-to-use jaggery powder. This makes a simplified ginger-spice sweet paste with a less developed flavor than the longer versions. Timing is an untested estimate; allow extra time if it still tastes raw or remains runny. Cooling is extra.

### Ingredients

- 2 tbsp fine jaggery powder
- ¼ tsp sukku / dry ginger powder
- ¼ tsp cumin powder
- A small pinch of black pepper powder
- A small pinch of omam / ajwain powder, optional
- 1 tsp ghee
- 2 tsp water, plus a few drops if needed

### Method

1. **Measure — about 1 minute:** Have everything ready beside a small, heavy-bottomed pan.
2. **Dissolve — 1–2 minutes:** Heat jaggery and water over low heat, stirring until dissolved. If your jaggery needs straining, strain now and allow extra time. No string consistency is needed.
3. **Cook — 3–4 minutes:** Stir in the spice powders and ghee. Keep stirring over low heat until the paste is glossy, the raw spice aroma fades, and a spoon leaves a brief trail. Add a few drops of water if it starts sticking before the spices cook.
4. Turn off the heat while the paste is still soft; it thickens as it cools. Let cool before tasting. Follow the serving and storage notes below.

**For this shortcut:** Skip fresh ginger, whole seeds, and woody roots. Do not increase the heat to rush thickening—the small quantity can scorch quickly. This is a culinary shortcut, not a medicinal equivalent of the traditional preparation.

## Simple version — about 15 minutes

A small, simplified Deepavali marundhu-style batch using kitchen spices and ready-ground powders. No soaking, fresh ginger, or woody roots are needed. The flavor will differ from the full traditional recipe below. This is an untested quick adaptation; cooking time depends on the pan and heat.

- **Preparation:** 3 minutes
- **Cooking:** 10–12 minutes; cooling is extra
- **Yield:** About ¼ cup

### Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Sukku / dry ginger powder | 1 tsp |
| Milagu / black pepper powder | ¼ tsp |
| Seeragam / cumin powder | ½ tsp |
| Dhaniya / coriander powder | ½ tsp |
| Omam / ajwain, finely powdered or crushed | ¼ tsp |
| Jaggery, finely grated or powdered | ¼ cup, loosely packed (about 40–45 g) |
| Ghee | 2 tsp |
| Water | 3 tbsp total, plus a little only if needed |
| Cardamom powder | A pinch, optional |

Use plain spice powders, not a curry or masala blend. For the quickest preparation, have the omam powdered before starting, or crush this small quantity finely in a mortar.

### Quick method

1. **Make a spice slurry — 2–3 minutes:** Mix dry ginger, pepper, cumin, coriander, and omam with **1 tbsp of the water** until smooth.
2. **Dissolve jaggery — 2–3 minutes:** Heat jaggery with the remaining **2 tbsp water** in a small, heavy-bottomed pan. Once dissolved, strain if needed and return the syrup to the pan. No string consistency is required.
3. **Cook — 6–8 minutes:** Stir in the spice slurry and **1 tsp ghee**. Cook over low to medium-low heat, stirring continuously, until the mixture thickens and loses its raw spice aroma. If it catches before the spices cook, add water a teaspoon at a time and continue; this may take longer.
4. **Finish — 1 minute:** Add the remaining **1 tsp ghee** and optional cardamom. Stir until glossy and soft, with a spoon leaving a brief trail. Stop while it is still spreadable; it thickens as it cools. Do not cook it into a firm ball just to meet the time estimate.
5. Let cool before tasting or transferring to a clean, dry jar. Follow the storage and serving notes below. This is a traditional food preparation, not a proven treatment.

**Shortcut note:** Ready-ground spices and powdered jaggery make the 15-minute target practical. Grinding whole roots or cooking fresh ginger belongs to the longer version below.

**Adaptation reference:** [Vidhya’s Vegetarian Kitchen — Deepavali Legiyam](https://www.vidhyashomecooking.com/diwali-legiyammarundhu/) describes cooking ground spices in dissolved jaggery. The smaller quantities, reduced ingredient list, and timing here are a separate quick adaptation.

## Full traditional version — 45–60 minutes

## Ingredients

### Traditional spices and roots

| Tamil name | English name | Quantity |
|---|---|---:|
| Sukku (சுக்கு) | Dry ginger | 15 g |
| Milagu (மிளகு) | Black pepper | 5 g |
| Seeragam (சீரகம்) | Cumin | 5 g |
| Dhaniya (தனியா) | Coriander seeds | 8 g |
| Omam (ஓமம்) | Ajwain | 3 g |
| Arisi thippili (அரிசி திப்பிலி) | Long pepper fruit | 3 g |
| Kandathippili (கண்டத்திப்பிலி) | Long pepper stem | 3 g |
| Chitharathai (சித்தரத்தை) | Lesser galangal | 3 g |
| Athimadhuram (அதிமதுரம்) | Licorice root | 2 g (optional; see safety note) |
| Elakkai (ஏலக்காய்) | Cardamom | 2 g |
| Krambu (கிராம்பு) | Cloves | 1 g |
| Pattai (பட்டை) | Cinnamon | 2 g |

### Fresh ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Fresh ginger, peeled | 75 g |
| Jaggery (vellam) | 175 g |
| Ghee | 30 g (approximately 2 tbsp) |
| Sesame oil (nallennai) | 5 g (approximately 1 tsp), optional |
| Water for grinding | 150–200 ml, as needed |
| Water for jaggery | 60 ml |

## Method

### 1. Clean and prepare

1. Break sukku, chitharathai, athimadhuram (if using), and kandathippili into small pieces using a mortar and pestle.
2. Check the spices for debris and remove any impurities.
3. Wash, peel, and finely chop the fresh ginger.

### 2. Dry roast the spices

1. Place a kadai over **very low heat**.
2. Add coriander, cumin, pepper, ajwain, both long peppers, cardamom, cloves, and cinnamon.
3. Roast gently for about **2–3 minutes**, stirring, until fragrant. Do not burn.
4. Warm the harder roots separately for **30–60 seconds**, if needed.
5. Cool completely.

### 3. Grind the spices and ginger

1. Grind the cooled dry spices and roots to a fine powder.
2. Sieve out coarse, woody pieces if necessary.
3. Add the fresh ginger and **150–200 ml water**, a little at a time.
4. Grind into a smooth, thick paste. Discard ungrindable fibers.

### 4. Prepare the jaggery syrup

1. Combine **175 g jaggery** and **60 ml water** in a small saucepan.
2. Heat gently until the jaggery dissolves.
3. Strain the syrup to remove impurities.
4. **No string consistency is required.**

### 5. Cook the ginger-spice paste

1. Heat about **1 tsp of the measured ghee** in a heavy-bottomed kadai.
2. Add the ground ginger-spice paste.
3. Cook on **low heat for 8–12 minutes**, stirring frequently, until the raw ginger aroma subsides and the paste thickens.
4. If the paste sticks, add a small splash of water.

### 6. Add jaggery and ghee

1. Pour the strained jaggery syrup into the cooked paste.
2. Stir continuously on low heat for **10–20 minutes**.
3. Add the remaining measured ghee in **2–3 portions**.
4. Add the optional sesame oil, if using.
5. Continue cooking until the mixture becomes dark brown, thick, and glossy.

### 7. Check the consistency

The marundhu is ready when it:

- Gathers into a soft mass.
- Begins leaving the sides of the pan.
- Holds its shape briefly on a spoon.
- Remains **soft and spreadable**, rather than hard or chewy.

Turn off the heat. It will thicken further on cooling.

## Traditional tips

- **Use a heavy-bottomed kadai** to avoid scorching.
- **Grind the woody roots very finely** for a smooth texture.
- **Keep the heat low** throughout cooking.
- **Do not overcook**: the legiyam firms up as it cools.
- Honey is not necessary for this recipe.
- Cool, transfer to a clean, dry airtight glass jar, refrigerate promptly, and use within approximately **7 days** as a conservative homemade-storage guideline. Always use a dry spoon.

## Traditional Deepavali serving

In many Tamil Brahmin households, a small amount of Deepavali Marundhu is eaten after the traditional oil bath and before festive sweets and savories. A small culinary portion for an adult is around **½ teaspoon**; this is **not a medically established dose**.

## Safety notes

- This is a traditional food preparation, **not a proven treatment** for indigestion or other conditions.
- **Licorice (athimadhuram)** can raise blood pressure, lower potassium, and interact with medications. Omit it if pregnant, or if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart disease, or potentially interacting medications, unless a qualified clinician advises otherwise.
- Concentrated herbal preparations may not be suitable for young children, pregnant people, or those taking medications; seek appropriate professional advice.
- Jaggery is a form of sugar and should be accounted for by people managing blood glucose.
- Avoid adding **vasambu (sweet flag)** without qualified guidance because of safety concerns.

## Recipe note

Tamil family recipes differ: some contain only sukku, milagu, thippili, omam, jaggery, and ghee, while others use many additional herbs. This recipe is a practical traditional-style variation, not a documented single-family heirloom formula.
