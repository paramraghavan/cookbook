## A. Fermentation, dairy, and sweets

### #31 — Ferment idli and dosa batter in the Instant Pot
1. Soak rice and urad dal, then grind to batter using your usual recipe (the source gives no rice-to-dal ratio).
2. Stir in salt, as described in the transcript.
3. Transfer to the steel inner pot, leaving ample headroom for expansion.
4. Rest the lid loosely, **without pressure sealing**.
5. Select **Yogurt** for **8–10 hours**.
6. Check for an expanded, pleasantly tangy batter; the transcript suggests approximately doubling.

**Why:** Steady warmth helps fermentation in cold weather. **Model note:** Yogurt settings differ; avoid settings hot enough to cook the batter.

### #28 — Make fresh paneer
**Ingredients:** ½ gallon whole milk; 3–4 tbsp lemon juice.
1. Heat milk on **Sauté**, stirring frequently, until steaming and beginning to foam.
2. Press **Cancel**. Stir in lemon juice.
3. Wait 1–2 minutes for curds to separate from greenish whey.
4. Pour through a cloth-lined strainer; rinse curds.
5. Wrap and press with a weight for **30 minutes**.

**Tip:** Save whey for kneading roti dough. **Safety:** Handle hot milk and whey carefully.

### #25 — Add dairy after pressure cooking
For vegetarian makhani, paneer gravies, and creamy dals, pressure-cook the non-dairy base first. After releasing pressure, whisk yogurt before adding it gradually, or stir in cream, milk, or butter. Warm gently using low Sauté. This reduces curdling, scorching, and burn warnings.

### #11 — Make homemade ghee
**Ingredient:** 1 lb unsalted butter.
1. Add butter to the inner pot and select **Low Sauté**, without a lid.
2. Simmer **20–30 minutes**, optionally skimming foam.
3. When bubbling quiets, milk solids become golden, and aroma turns nutty, switch off heat.
4. Strain through a cloth-lined sieve into a clean, dry jar.

**Watch closely:** Ghee can go from golden to burnt quickly.

### #8 — Make rice kheer without scorching milk
**Ingredients:** ¼ cup rinsed basmati rice, 1 cup water, 4 cups whole milk; sugar, cardamom, chopped nuts, and optional saffron to taste (amounts not specified).
1. Pressure-cook rice and water on **High for ~3 minutes**.
2. Release pressure (method not specified in transcript).
3. Add milk; simmer on **Sauté for 15–20 minutes**, stirring often.
4. Add sugar, cardamom, nuts, and optional saffron soaked in warm milk.

**Why:** Cooking rice in water before adding milk helps avoid milk scorching under pressure.

### #1 — Make homemade dahi (yogurt)
**Ingredients:** ½ gallon whole milk; 2 tbsp plain yogurt with live cultures.
1. Heat milk on Sauté, stirring, to **180°F (82°C)**.
2. Cool to approximately **110°F (43°C)**.
3. Gently whisk in the starter yogurt.
4. Cover and select **Yogurt for 8–10 hours**.
5. Refrigerate before using; reserve a little as the next batch's starter.

**Why:** Heating improves texture; cooling protects live cultures.

## B. Dal, beans, rice, and one-pot meals

### #30 — Dark restaurant-style chole with black tea
**Ingredients:** Dried chickpeas, water (~3× chickpea volume), salt, 1 plain black tea bag, and chole masala (amount not specified).
1. Add unsoaked chickpeas, water, salt, and a plain black tea bag.
2. Pressure-cook on **High for ~50 minutes**.
3. Allow **natural pressure release**.
4. Remove tea bag, then add your prepared masala.

**Why:** Tea darkens the chickpeas; pressure softens unsoaked beans. **Note:** Check chickpeas are fully tender; large/old beans may take longer.

### #29 — Cook rice and dal together, pot-in-pot
1. Add dal, water, and turmeric to the main pot.
2. Set a trivet over the dal.
3. Place a heatproof bowl containing rinsed rice and its cooking water on the trivet.
4. Pressure-cook on **High for ~10 minutes**; the transcript suggests a little longer for deep/thick bowls.

**Why:** Rice and dal cook separately in the same appliance. **Important:** Exact dal/rice ratios and release method are not specified; choose compatible cooking times and ensure the main pot has adequate thin liquid.

### #24 — Reduce foaming when pressure-cooking dal and beans
- Add approximately **1 tsp oil or ghee**.
- Keep the cooker **at or below half full** for expanding/foaming ingredients.
- Skim visible froth before sealing if needed.

**Why:** Starchy foam can interfere with venting. **Safety:** Follow the manufacturer's fill limits and release guidance; oil is not a substitute for safe fill levels.

### #23 — No-soak dal timing guide
| Dal | Water stated in source | High-pressure time | Release |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toor dal | 1 cup dal : ~3 cups water | 8–10 min | Natural 10 min |
| Masoor (red) | 1 cup dal : ~2½ cups water | 4–5 min | Natural 10 min |
| Moong dal | Not given | ~5 min | Natural 10 min |
| Chana dal | Not given | ~15 min | Natural 10 min |

After cooking, whisk or blend for a smoother consistency. These are starting points, not fixed rules.

### #22 — Convert stovetop cooker whistles cautiously
The transcript proposes **three whistles ≈ 8–10 minutes on High for dal** as a rough starting point. It also discusses meat, omitted here. **Editorial caution:** There is no universal whistle-to-minute conversion; adjust for ingredient, quantity, cooker, altitude, and desired texture. Record the result for future batches.

### #21 — Make a finishing tadka using Sauté
1. Transfer cooked dal to a serving bowl.
2. Heat ghee or oil in the empty inner pot on **Sauté**.
3. Add mustard or cumin seeds; let sizzle.
4. Add dried red chilies, curry leaves, hing, and sliced garlic; cook about **30 seconds**, monitoring to prevent burning.
5. Pour the sizzling tadka over the dal.

**Why:** Hot fat releases spice aromas. **Safety:** Dry curry leaves thoroughly to minimize splattering.

### #20 — Basmati rice, 1:1 method
**Ingredients:** 1 cup basmati rice, 1 cup water, pinch of salt, 1 tsp ghee.
1. Rinse rice until water runs mostly clear.
2. Optionally soak **15–20 minutes**, then drain thoroughly.
3. Add rice, water, salt, and ghee.
4. Pressure-cook on **High for 4 minutes**.
5. **Natural release for 10 minutes**, then fluff.

**Note:** Rice variety, age, soaking, and desired texture may require adjustment.

### #19 — Layered vegetable biryani
1. Put seasoned/marinated vegetables and fried onions in the bottom with enough thin liquid.
2. Spread soaked basmati rice on top in an even layer; **do not stir**.
3. Add water and pressure-cook on **High for ~5 minutes**.
4. **Natural release for 10 minutes**; gently fold layers together.

**Source limitation:** The transcript combines chicken and vegetable versions and gives a liquid guideline based on chicken juices. That liquid guideline **does not directly apply to vegetable biryani**. The exact vegetarian water ratio and vegetable quantities are not provided; use a tested vegetarian biryani recipe for those details.

### #14 — Cook rajma before adding tomato masala
1. Soak kidney beans overnight, or use unsoaked beans with a longer cooking time.
2. Pressure-cook with water and salt on **High for 25–30 minutes (soaked)** or **~45 minutes (unsoaked)**.
3. Let pressure release naturally.
4. Verify beans are **completely cooked and tender**.
5. Stir in onion-tomato masala and simmer on **Sauté for ~10 minutes**.

**Why:** Acidic tomato ingredients may slow softening. **Food safety:** Raw or undercooked red kidney beans contain harmful lectins. Follow a validated bean-cooking method and never serve beans that remain undercooked; pressure times alone do not guarantee safety for every appliance and batch.

### #13 — One-pot moong dal khichdi
**Ingredients:** ½ cup rice, ½ cup split yellow moong dal, 4 cups water, turmeric, salt, 1 tsp ghee.
1. Rinse rice and moong dal together.
2. Add water, turmeric, salt, and ghee.
3. Pressure-cook on **High for 8 minutes**.
4. Allow **natural pressure release**.
5. Stir; add hot water for a softer or soupier texture.

### #18 — Steam potatoes for aloo dishes
1. Add **1 cup water** to the inner pot and insert a trivet.
2. Place whole, unpeeled potatoes on the trivet.
3. Pressure-cook on **High for ~8 minutes (small)** or **12–15 minutes (medium)**.
4. Use quick release as described in the source, then cool before peeling.

**Uses:** Samosa filling, aloo paratha, dry aloo sabzi. Check doneness and adjust for potato size.

### #12 — Steam idlis
1. Add **1½ cups water** to the inner pot.
2. Grease the idli stand, fill molds with fermented batter, and lower stand into pot.
3. The transcript says to use **Steam with the valve at Venting for 10–12 minutes**.
4. Switch off, wait 2 minutes, and test an idli with a toothpick.

**Model caution:** Steam behavior varies between Instant Pot models. On some, the Steam program requires pressure sealing or will not operate as described with venting. Follow your model's manual or use a tested no-pressure steaming method.

## C. Vegetable curries, bases, and flavor techniques

### #27 — Prevent the Burn warning by layering
1. After sautéing onions, add about **½ cup water** and thoroughly scrape up stuck bits (deglaze).
2. Add other ingredients with enough thin liquid for your specific model and recipe.
3. Place thick tomato puree/paste or coconut paste on top, **without stirring it down to the base**.

The transcript suggests **at least 1 cup of thin liquid**; this is not universal across cooker sizes and recipes. Follow your model's minimum-liquid requirements.

### #26 — Add finishing aromatics after pressure cooking
After opening the cooker, stir in **garam masala, crushed kasuri methi, fresh cilantro, and lemon juice**. Briefly simmer on **Sauté for 1–2 minutes** if appropriate. Adding them late preserves fresh aroma.

### #16 — Make a master onion-tomato masala and freeze cubes
**Ingredients:** 1 chopped onion, 4–5 tomatoes, a large knob of ginger, 6 garlic cloves, 2 green chilies, ½ cup water; oil, turmeric, and salt (quantities not specified).
1. Pressure-cook onion, tomatoes, ginger, garlic, chilies, and water on **High for 5 minutes**.
2. Release pressure (method not stated); blend until smooth.
3. Sauté with oil, turmeric, and salt until thickened.
4. Cool promptly, portion into ice cube trays, and freeze.

**Use:** Add one or two cubes to a quick curry base, adjusting to the dish. **Note:** The source gives no exact cube size or storage duration.

### #10 — One-pot pav bhaji
**Ingredients:** Chopped potatoes, cauliflower, carrots, peas, tomato, 1 cup water, salt, turmeric, pav bhaji masala; butter for finishing (vegetable amounts not specified).
1. Combine vegetables, water, and spices.
2. Pressure-cook on **High for 6 minutes**.
3. Quick-release as directed in the source.
4. Select **Sauté**, add butter, and mash directly in the pot.
5. Simmer until thick; serve with toasted, buttered pav or rolls.

### #9 — Keep palak paneer bright green
1. Pressure-cook onions, tomato, ginger, garlic, spices, and approximately **1 cup water** to make the base (**time not specified**).
2. Release pressure.
3. Add fresh spinach and wilt on **Sauté for 2–3 minutes**.
4. Blend with an immersion blender.
5. Fold in cubed paneer or a little cream.

**Why:** Brief spinach cooking helps preserve green color. **Source limitation:** No ingredient quantities or pressure time for the base are given.

### #3 — Use a removable whole-spice pouch
Place cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, bay leaves, and peppercorns in a heat-safe stainless-steel tea infuser or suitable muslin pouch. Add it during cooking and remove before serving. The source also suggests this for masala chai prepared on Sauté, adding milk toward the end.

## D. Cleaning, holding, and scheduling

### #7 — Remove curry smells from the sealing ring
Remove the silicone ring and soak in warm water with a splash of white vinegar for about **1 hour**, or wash on the dishwasher's top rack if manufacturer-approved. The source also suggests steaming water, vinegar, and lemon peels for **2 minutes** to freshen the pot. A separate ring for sweet dishes can help prevent flavor transfer. Follow the appliance's cleaning guidance.

### #6 — Fade turmeric stains
Wash removable stained parts normally, then expose appropriate parts to sunlight for a few hours. For stainless steel, the source suggests baking soda with water, either as a paste or gentle simmer. Avoid abrasive treatment of components where prohibited by the manufacturer.

### #5 — Use Keep Warm when hosting
Keep cooked curries, dal, or rice warm for serving. The source cites **140°F (60°C)** as a hot-holding target. **Safety:** Confirm actual food temperature with a thermometer if holding for extended periods; a Keep Warm label alone does not establish safe temperature. Avoid prolonged rice holding for quality and food-safety reasons.

### #4 — Use Delay Start cautiously
The transcript suggests delayed cooking for dry rice, lentils, and beans with water and seasonings. **Safety clarification:** Once water is added, ingredients are no longer dry. Avoid long room-temperature delays, especially in warm kitchens; follow your Instant Pot model's instructions and food-safety recommendations. Never delay-start perishable dairy or other temperature-sensitive foods.

## E. Practical cooking checklist

- [ ] Verify the recipe's minimum thin-liquid requirement for your Instant Pot size.
- [ ] Keep foaming beans and lentils below the **half-full** limit.
- [ ] Deglaze after Sauté before pressure cooking.
- [ ] Layer thick tomato/coconut pastes on top rather than against the bottom.
- [ ] Check whether the recipe calls for natural or quick pressure release; don't guess for foamy foods.
- [ ] Add delicate herbs, garam masala, kasuri methi, and dairy after pressure cooking when appropriate.
- [ ] Check beans for full doneness, especially kidney beans.
- [ ] Follow model-specific instructions for Yogurt, Steam, and Delay Start.
- [ ] Cool and refrigerate/freeze leftovers promptly.
