# Kung Pao Tofu — Simple Home Version

## Ingredients

* 14–16 oz **extra-firm tofu**
* 1 tbsp cornstarch
* 1–2 tbsp oil
* 1 bell pepper, cut into pieces
* ⅓–½ cup roasted peanuts
* 3–5 dried red chilies, broken into pieces
* 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped
* 1–2 green onions, sliced — optional

## Sauce

Mix together:

* 2 tbsp soy sauce
* **2 tsp white vinegar**
* 1 tbsp sugar or brown sugar
* 1 tsp sesame oil — optional
* **¼ cup water**
* 1 tsp cornstarch

---

# 1. Prepare the Tofu

Drain and pat the tofu **very dry**.

Cut into about ¾-inch cubes.

Toss gently with:

* 1 tbsp cornstarch
* 1–2 tsp oil
* Small pinch of salt

---

# 2. Crisp the Tofu — Choose One Method

## Option A — Oven

1. Preheat oven to **425°F / 220°C**.
2. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper or lightly oil it.
3. Spread tofu in a **single layer**, leaving a little space between pieces.
4. Bake for **25–30 minutes**.
5. Flip the tofu after about **15 minutes**.
6. Remove when the edges are golden and crisp.

**Tip:** For extra crisp tofu, bake another 3–5 minutes if needed.

## Option B — Skillet

1. Heat 1–2 tbsp oil in a wide skillet over **medium-high heat**.
2. Add tofu in a single layer.
3. Cook for **7–10 minutes**, turning every couple of minutes.
4. Remove when golden and crisp on several sides.

**Skillet:** faster and slightly richer.

**Oven:** easier, less oil, and more hands-off.

---

# 3. Cook the Vegetables

Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat.

Add a little oil if the pan is dry.

Add:

1. Bell pepper → cook **1–2 minutes**
2. Dried red chilies → cook **15–20 seconds**
3. Garlic → cook **20–30 seconds**

Do not let the garlic or chilies burn.

---

# 4. Add the Sauce

Stir the sauce again because the cornstarch settles.

Pour it into the skillet.

Cook for **30–60 seconds**, stirring, until it becomes slightly thick and glossy.

---

# 5. Finish

Add:

* Crispy tofu
* Peanuts
* Green onion

Toss gently for about **30–60 seconds**, just until everything is coated.

Turn off the heat.

---

# Taste Check

Kung Pao should taste:

**savory + slightly sweet + tangy + spicy**

Adjust if needed:

* More soy sauce → saltier
* A few drops white vinegar → tangier
* A little sugar → sweeter
* More dried chili or chili flakes → spicier

Serve immediately with steamed rice.

# Important Tips

* Dry the tofu very well before adding cornstarch.
* Don't overcrowd the baking sheet or skillet.
* **425°F works better than a lower oven temperature** for crisp tofu.
* Flip oven-baked tofu halfway through.
* Add tofu to the sauce only at the very end.
* Don't simmer the tofu in the sauce for several minutes or it will soften.
* Add peanuts near the end so they stay crunchy.

## Optional

For a more traditional Kung Pao flavor, add **½–1 tsp crushed Sichuan peppercorns** with the dried chilies.
> use the **oven method** if you want easier cleanup and consistently crisp tofu; 
> use the skillet when you want it done faster.
