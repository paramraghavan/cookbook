# Kung Pao Tofu — Simple Home Version

## Ingredients

* 14–16 oz **extra-firm tofu**
* 1 tbsp cornstarch
* 1–2 tbsp oil
* 1 bell pepper, cut into pieces
* ⅓–½ cup roasted peanuts
* 3–5 dried red chilies, broken into pieces
* 2 cloves garlic, finely chopped
* 1–2 green onions, sliced — optional

## Sauce

Mix together:

* 2 tbsp soy sauce
* **2 tsp white vinegar**
* 1 tbsp sugar or brown sugar
* 1 tsp sesame oil — optional but recommended
* **¼ cup water**
* 1 tsp cornstarch

## 1. Prepare the tofu

Drain and pat the tofu **very dry**.

Cut into ¾-inch cubes.

Toss gently with:

* 1 tbsp cornstarch
* Small pinch of salt

## 2. Crisp the tofu

Heat 1–2 tbsp oil in a wide skillet over **medium-high heat**.

Add tofu in a single layer.

Cook for **7–10 minutes**, turning occasionally, until golden and crisp on several sides.

Remove to a plate.

## 3. Cook the aromatics

In the same pan, add:

1. Bell pepper → cook **1–2 minutes**
2. Dried red chilies → cook **15–20 seconds**
3. Garlic → cook another **20–30 seconds**

Don't let the garlic or chilies burn.

## 4. Add the sauce

Stir the sauce again because the cornstarch settles.

Pour it into the hot pan.

Cook for about **30–60 seconds**, stirring, until it becomes slightly thick and glossy.

## 5. Finish

Add:

* Crispy tofu
* Peanuts
* Green onion

Toss gently for about **30–60 seconds**, just until everything is coated.

Turn off the heat.

## Taste Check

Kung Pao should taste:

**savory + slightly sweet + tangy + spicy**

Adjust if needed:

* More soy sauce → saltier
* A few drops white vinegar → tangier
* A little sugar → sweeter
* More dried chili/chili flakes → spicier

Serve immediately with steamed rice.

## Important Tips

* **Dry the tofu well** before frying.
* Don't overcrowd the pan.
* Don't cook garlic for long.
* With **white vinegar, start with less** because it is quite sharp.
* Add the crispy tofu only at the end so it doesn't become soggy.
* Peanuts should stay slightly crunchy, so add them near the end.

One optional ingredient that will make the flavor noticeably closer to Chinese Kung Pao is **½–1 tsp freshly ground
Sichuan peppercorn**. But even without it, this version should come out very good.
