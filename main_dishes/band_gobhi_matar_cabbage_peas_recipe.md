# Band Gobhi Matar (North Indian Cabbage and Peas Sabzi)

A simple, dry-style North Indian cabbage stir-fry with sweet, lightly golden edges, green peas, cumin, ginger, and a bright lemon finish. The secret is to cook **without adding water or covering the pan**, allowing the cabbage's natural moisture to evaporate rather than steam it.

- **Cuisine:** North Indian
- **Diet:** Vegetarian, vegan
- **Servings:** 4 as a side dish
- **Approximate cooking time:** 15–20 minutes, plus preparation
- **Method:** Open-pan stir-fry

## Ingredients

### Vegetables and aromatics

- 1 small green cabbage (about 1½ lb / 680 g), cored and finely shredded
- 1 cup frozen green peas
- 1 green chili, finely chopped (adjust to taste)
- 1-inch piece fresh ginger, grated (about 1 tablespoon)
- Chopped fresh cilantro, for garnish

### Oil and spices

- 3 tablespoons oil
- 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
- ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
- 1 teaspoon ground coriander
- ½ teaspoon red chili powder (adjust to taste)
- About ¾ teaspoon salt, or to taste
- ½ teaspoon garam masala

### Finishing touch

- A squeeze of fresh lemon juice **or** ½ teaspoon amchur (dried mango powder)

## Equipment

- Wide, heavy-bottomed frying pan or skillet (preferably large enough to spread the cabbage into a relatively thin layer)
- Sharp knife or mandoline
- Spatula

## Preparation

1. **Clean and dry the cabbage:** Rinse the cabbage while whole, then dry thoroughly. Remove the core and any damaged outer leaves.
2. **Shred thinly:** Slice into thin, even strips so the cabbage cooks quickly and browns at the edges.
3. **Prepare aromatics:** Chop the green chili, grate the ginger, and measure the spices.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### 1. Temper the cumin

1. Heat **3 tablespoons oil** in a wide pan over medium to medium-high heat.
2. Add **1 teaspoon cumin seeds** and let them sizzle until fragrant and lightly toasted. Do not burn them.
3. Add the chopped green chili and grated ginger. Sauté for **30 seconds**.
4. Add turmeric, ground coriander, and red chili powder. Stir for **10–15 seconds**, keeping the spices from scorching.

### 2. Cook the cabbage uncovered

1. Add all the shredded cabbage to the pan and toss thoroughly with the seasoned oil.
2. Cook over medium-high heat **without a lid and without adding water**.
3. Stir approximately once per minute, spreading the cabbage back across the pan after each stir to encourage moisture evaporation and light browning.
4. After **5–6 minutes**, when the cabbage has wilted and the first released moisture has mostly evaporated, add **about ¾ teaspoon salt**.

> **Why salt halfway through?** Salt draws moisture from vegetables. Waiting until the first moisture has evaporated can help the cabbage retain a drier, lightly browned texture. Salting earlier is also a valid technique, but the cabbage may release water sooner.

### 3. Add the peas

1. Continue cooking until the cabbage edges begin turning lightly golden, roughly **10 minutes total** from when the cabbage was added. Timing depends on the pan, heat, and cabbage quantity.
2. Add **1 cup frozen peas** directly to the pan.
3. Stir-fry for **3–4 minutes**, until the peas are heated through and tender and the cabbage is cooked but not soggy.

### 4. Finish and serve

1. Turn off the heat or reduce it to low.
2. Add **½ teaspoon garam masala**.
3. Finish with a squeeze of **lemon juice** or **½ teaspoon amchur**.
4. Taste and adjust salt or acidity.
5. Garnish with fresh cilantro and serve hot.

## Key Tips for the Best Texture

- **Do not add water:** Cabbage naturally contains plenty of moisture.
- **Do not cover the pan:** Trapped steam makes the cabbage soft instead of lightly golden.
- **Use a wide pan:** More surface area helps moisture escape. Cook in two batches if the pan is crowded.
- **Cut the cabbage thinly:** Thin, even shreds cook quickly and consistently.
- **Stir periodically, not constantly:** Letting cabbage contact the hot pan briefly helps the edges color.
- **Salt after initial wilting:** For this version, add salt once the first released moisture has cooked off.
- **Finish with garam masala and acid:** Garam masala adds aroma; lemon or amchur brightens the flavor.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| Cabbage turns soggy | Remove the lid if used, increase heat moderately, and cook uncovered until excess liquid evaporates. Next time, use a wider pan or smaller batches. |
| Spices or cabbage scorch on the bottom | Lower the heat to medium. Add a spoonful of water only if needed to loosen stuck bits, then let it evaporate. |
| Flavor tastes flat | Add a pinch of salt and a squeeze of lemon juice. |
| Cabbage doesn't brown | Spread it out more, let liquid evaporate, and avoid excessive stirring. |

## Variations

- **Cabbage and potato:** Add a small, finely cubed potato early in the cooking process. Cook until the potato is tender before finishing; adjust timing as needed.
- **Cabbage and tomato:** Add one chopped tomato along with the peas. The dish will be slightly more moist.
- **Extra spice:** Increase the green chili or red chili powder to taste.

## Serving Suggestions

Enjoy with roti, chapati, paratha, dal and rice, or as a vegetable side in a North Indian thali.

## Storage and Reheating

- Refrigerate leftovers within **2 hours** in a covered container.
- Eat within **3–4 days**.
- Reheat thoroughly in a hot, mostly dry pan to help remove extra moisture.
