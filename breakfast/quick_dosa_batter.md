# Quick Dosa Batter (Soak Everything Together)

An easy dosa batter made by soaking rice, dals, and fenugreek seeds together and grinding them in one batch.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Idli rice (or dosa rice) | 3 cups |
| Urad dal (split, skinned) | 1 cup |
| Chana dal | 2 tablespoons |
| Toor dal | 2 tablespoons |
| Methi (fenugreek) seeds | 1 teaspoon |
| Water | As needed for soaking and grinding |
| Salt | To taste |

## Steps

1. **Rinse:** Combine the rice, urad dal, chana dal, toor dal, and methi seeds in a large bowl. Wash 3–4 times until the water is mostly clear.
2. **Soak:** Cover with plenty of fresh water and soak for **4–6 hours**.
3. **Drain:** Drain the soaking water. Reserve a little if desired, or use fresh cold water for grinding.
4. **Grind together:** Add everything to a wet grinder or powerful blender. Gradually add water and grind to a **smooth, thick, pourable batter**. Avoid making it watery; if using a blender, grind in batches to prevent overheating.
5. **Ferment:** Transfer to a large container, leaving room for the batter to rise. Cover loosely and leave in a warm place for **8–12 hours**, or until slightly risen and bubbly. It can take longer in cold weather.
6. **Add salt and adjust:** Stir in salt to taste. Add small amounts of water if needed to reach a flowing dosa consistency.
7. **Make dosas:** Heat a tawa or nonstick pan. Pour a ladleful of batter in the center and spread outward into a thin circle. Drizzle a little oil around the edges. Cook until the bottom is golden and crisp; flip briefly if preferred.

## Tips

- **Crispier dosas:** Spread the batter thinly on a properly heated pan. The chana dal and toor dal can help with a golden color.
- **Fermentation:** The batter should smell pleasantly tangy, not rotten. Use a sufficiently large container because it can expand.
- **Storage:** Refrigerate fermented batter in a covered container and use within **3–4 days** for best quality. Stir gently before using.
- **Timing:** Soaking and fermentation take time, but the hands-on steps are simple.
