Wagh Bakri Ginger–Cardamom Masala Chai

A rich, aromatic Indian milk chai with fresh ginger, green cardamom, and warming spices.

Yield: 2 cups
Time: About 12 minutes

Ingredients

|Ingredient                          |Quantity         |
|------------------------------------|----------------:|
|Wagh Bakri CTC tea                  |2½ tsp           |
|Whole milk                          |1½ cups          |
|Water                               |1 cup            |
|Fresh ginger, crushed               |1-inch piece     |
|Green cardamom pods, lightly crushed|3                |
|Cinnamon stick                      |½-inch piece     |
|Clove                               |1                |
|Black peppercorns (optional)        |2                |
|Sugar                               |2–3 tsp, to taste|

> Use regular Wagh Bakri Premium Leaf Tea (CTC), rather than a premixed masala chai. The starting liquid quantity is 2½ cups; some evaporates during cooking.

Instructions

1. Crush the aromatics. Lightly crush the ginger and cardamom pods with a mortar and pestle. Break the cinnamon into a small piece.
2. Simmer the spices (3 minutes). Bring 1 cup water to a boil in a small saucepan. Add ginger, cardamom, cinnamon, clove, and optional peppercorns. Simmer over medium heat for 3 minutes.
3. Brew the tea (45–60 seconds). Add 2½ teaspoons Wagh Bakri CTC tea. Boil gently until the water turns deep reddish-brown; avoid overboiling at this stage.
4. Add milk and sugar (4–5 minutes). Add 1½ cups whole milk and 2–3 teaspoons sugar. Bring just to a gentle boil, watching closely to prevent overflow. Lower the heat and simmer for 3–4 minutes, stirring occasionally.
5. Strain and serve. Turn off the heat, let the chai settle for about 20 seconds, and strain into warmed cups. Serve hot.

Secrets for Better Chai

• Crush fresh ginger instead of merely slicing it for a stronger aroma.
• Use cardamom generously but cloves sparingly: one clove is enough for two cups.
• Whole milk gives a rich, creamy mouthfeel.
• Do not boil CTC tea in water for too long or it can become bitter.
• Try ⅛ teaspoon fennel seeds for a delicate sweet aroma.

Adjustments

|Style                |Tea   |Milk   |Water  |Milk-simmer time|
|---------------------|-----:|------:|------:|---------------:|
|Mild and milky       |1¾ tsp|1¾ cups|¾ cup  |3 minutes       |
|Balanced and aromatic|2½ tsp|1½ cups|1 cup  |3–4 minutes     |
|Strong *kadak* chai  |3½ tsp|1¼ cups|1¼ cups|5 minutes       |

Sugar options for two cups: none (0 tsp), lightly sweet (1 tsp), or traditional sweetness (about 2½ tsp). Adjust to taste.

Optional South Indian-Inspired Variation

Add ¼ teaspoon lightly crushed fennel seeds and a tiny pinch of dried ginger powder (sukku) to the simmering water for a rounder, fragrant chai.

Scaling

For one cup, halve the ingredients. For four cups, double them. Keep an eye on the saucepan because milk rises quickly as it boils.
